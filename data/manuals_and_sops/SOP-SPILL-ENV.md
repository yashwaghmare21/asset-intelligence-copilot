# Standard Operating Procedure: Environmental Safety & Spill Compliance
**Document ID:** SOP-ENV-SPILL-COMPLIANCE-2024  
**Regulatory Code:** 40 CFR Part 112 / Regional Energy Board Rule 7A  
**Classification:** Environmental Health, Safety & Regulatory Mandate

## 1. Spill Incident Definition & Thresholds
During weekly physical and aerial/drone inspections, field personnel and autonomous systems inspect the containment berm, wellhead apron, and flowline perimeter for liquid accumulation.

### Critical Regulatory Thresholds:
* **Reportable Spill Threshold:** Any crude oil release reaching or exceeding **5.0 barrels (bbl)** (approx. 210 US gallons) is legally classified as a **Major Reportable Incident**.
* **Statutory Notification Window:** Spills $\ge 5.0\text{ bbl}$ must be formally reported to the State/Federal Energy Commission & Environmental Regulator within **24 hours** of discovery.
* **Failure to Report Penalty:** Failure to submit notification within 24 hours results in automatic Class II Administrative Violations, mandatory site audits, and non-negotiable statutory fines starting at $15,000 per violation.
* **Minor Spills (< 5.0 bbl):** Logged in internal containment registry; local remedial clean-up required within 72 hours, but no mandatory 24-hr regulator notification is triggered unless surface waterways are reached.

## 2. Field Corrections & Human Verification Protocol ("Correction Wins Rule")
* **Initial Inspection Uncertainty:** Visual inspection or drone multispectral analysis may occasionally misidentify stagnant rainwater, drilling mud runoff, or hydraulic fluid as a crude oil spill.
* **Correction Override Authority:** If certified field operators, environmental coordinators, or senior technicians perform a physical ground-truth inspection and record a correction note (e.g., *"Initial spill report on WELL-0045 was clarified to be 0.0 bbl rainwater puddle"*), **the human ground-truth correction strictly supersedes the initial drone/inspection record**.
* **Audit Trail Requirement:** The copilot and operational database must preserve the initial raw inspection record, capture the identity and timestamp of the correcting technician, and recalculate all compliance deadlines and penalties using the corrected volume.
